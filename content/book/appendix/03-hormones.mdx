Endocrine questions on the MCAT rarely ask for a hormone in isolation. They describe a patient or an experiment, such as a tumor, a knockout mouse or an injected drug, and ask what happens downstream. To answer them, you need four things for each hormone: where it comes from, what kind of molecule it is, what it acts on, and what turns it on or off. The tables below are organized to give you exactly that.

Chemical class is listed as **peptide** (including protein and glycoprotein hormones), **steroid** (derived from cholesterol) or **amine** (derived from a single amino acid, tyrosine or tryptophan). The final section explains why the class matters.

## How Hormones Signal: Peptide, Steroid and Amine

A hormone's chemical class largely determines how it travels in the blood, where its receptor is and how fast it acts.

| Property | Peptide hormones | Steroid hormones | Amine hormones |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built from | Amino acid chains, made as larger precursors (preprohormones) and cleaved | Cholesterol | Tyrosine (catecholamines, thyroid hormones) or tryptophan (melatonin) |
| Synthesis and storage | Made on the rough ER, packaged by the Golgi, stored in vesicles, released by exocytosis | Made in the smooth ER and mitochondria on demand; not stored, they diffuse out as soon as they're made | Catecholamines are stored in vesicles; thyroid hormones are stored in the thyroid as part of thyroglobulin |
| Solubility | Water-soluble | Lipid-soluble | Catecholamines are water-soluble; thyroid hormones are lipid-soluble |
| Transport in blood | Mostly dissolved freely | Bound to carrier proteins (such as albumin or sex hormone–binding globulin) | Catecholamines travel free; thyroid hormones are mostly bound (for example, to thyroxine-binding globulin) |
| Receptor location | Cell surface | Inside the cell (cytoplasm or nucleus) | Catecholamines: cell surface. Thyroid hormones: nucleus |
| Mechanism | Receptor activates a second-messenger cascade (cAMP, IP₃/DAG, Ca²⁺) or a receptor tyrosine kinase | Hormone–receptor complex binds DNA and acts as a transcription factor | Catecholamines act like peptides (G protein–coupled receptors); thyroid hormones act like steroids |
| Speed and duration | Fast onset, short-lived effects | Slower onset, longer-lasting effects | Catecholamines: fast and brief. Thyroid hormones: slow and prolonged |
| Examples | Insulin, glucagon, ADH, PTH, the pituitary hormones | Cortisol, aldosterone, estrogen, testosterone, progesterone | Epinephrine, norepinephrine, T₃/T₄, melatonin |

<Callout type="high-yield" title="Thyroid hormone is the exception to watch">
T₃ and T₄ are amines, but they behave like steroids: they cross the cell membrane, bind nuclear receptors and change gene transcription. Catecholamines are also amines, but they behave like peptides. On the exam, think about solubility and receptor location rather than the class name alone.
</Callout>

### Tropic and direct hormones

A **tropic** hormone acts on another endocrine gland, causing it to release its own hormone. A **direct** hormone acts on non-endocrine target tissue. Most hypothalamic hormones and four of the anterior pituitary hormones (FSH, LH, ACTH and TSH) are tropic.

### Negative feedback

Most endocrine axes are regulated by **negative feedback**: the final hormone in the chain inhibits release of the hormones upstream of it. For example, high cortisol suppresses both CRH and ACTH. The classic exam move is to ask what happens to each hormone in an axis when one gland is overactive or removed.

## Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus links the nervous and endocrine systems. It releases hormones into the hypophyseal portal system, which carries them directly to the anterior pituitary. It also makes ADH and oxytocin, which travel down axons to be released from the posterior pituitary.

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) | Hypothalamus | Peptide | Anterior pituitary | Stimulates TSH release (also stimulates prolactin) | Inhibited by T₃/T₄ (negative feedback); stimulated by cold |
| Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) | Hypothalamus | Peptide | Anterior pituitary | Stimulates ACTH release | Stimulated by stress and daily rhythm; inhibited by cortisol |
| Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) | Hypothalamus | Peptide | Anterior pituitary | Stimulates FSH and LH release | Released in pulses; inhibited by sex steroids and by high prolactin |
| Growth hormone–releasing hormone (GHRH) | Hypothalamus | Peptide | Anterior pituitary | Stimulates GH release | Stimulated by sleep, low blood glucose, exercise; inhibited by GH and IGF-1 |
| Somatostatin (growth hormone–inhibiting hormone) | Hypothalamus (also pancreatic δ cells and the gut) | Peptide | Anterior pituitary | Inhibits GH and TSH release | Stimulated by GH and IGF-1 |
| Dopamine (prolactin-inhibiting hormone) | Hypothalamus | Amine | Anterior pituitary | Inhibits prolactin release | Reduced by suckling, which lets prolactin rise |

## Anterior Pituitary

The anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) is true glandular tissue. It releases seven hormones, almost all under the control of hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones.

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) | Anterior pituitary | Peptide (glycoprotein) | Ovaries (granulosa cells); testes (Sertoli cells) | Follicle development; spermatogenesis | Stimulated by GnRH; inhibited by inhibin and sex steroids |
| Luteinizing hormone (LH) | Anterior pituitary | Peptide (glycoprotein) | Ovaries (theca cells); testes (Leydig cells) | Triggers ovulation and corpus luteum formation; stimulates testosterone production | Stimulated by GnRH; LH surge triggered by high estrogen (positive feedback); otherwise inhibited by sex steroids |
| Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) | Anterior pituitary | Peptide (cleaved from POMC) | Adrenal cortex | Stimulates glucocorticoid (cortisol) release | Stimulated by CRH; inhibited by cortisol |
| Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) | Anterior pituitary | Peptide (glycoprotein) | Thyroid | Stimulates T₃/T₄ synthesis and release; thyroid growth | Stimulated by TRH; inhibited by T₃/T₄ and somatostatin |
| Prolactin | Anterior pituitary | Peptide | Mammary glands | Milk **production** | Inhibited by dopamine; increased by suckling and during pregnancy; high prolactin suppresses GnRH |
| Endorphins | Anterior pituitary (also the CNS) | Peptide (cleaved from POMC) | Brain (opioid receptors) | Reduce pain perception; promote euphoria | Released in stress, pain and exercise |
| Growth hormone (GH, somatotropin) | Anterior pituitary | Peptide | Liver, bone, muscle, many tissues | Promotes growth (largely via IGF-1 from the liver); raises blood glucose by reducing glucose uptake; increases lipolysis and protein synthesis | Stimulated by GHRH, sleep and exercise; inhibited by somatostatin and IGF-1 |

<Callout type="mnemonic" title="FLAT PEG">
The anterior pituitary hormones: **F**SH, **L**H, **A**CTH, **T**SH, **P**rolactin, **E**ndorphins, **G**rowth hormone. The **FLAT** hormones are tropic; the **PEG** hormones are direct.
</Callout>

## Posterior Pituitary

The posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) is neural tissue. It doesn't make hormones: it stores and releases two peptides made in the hypothalamus's supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei.

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin) | Made in the hypothalamus; released from the posterior pituitary | Peptide | Kidney collecting ducts; vascular smooth muscle | Inserts aquaporins in the collecting duct, so more water is reabsorbed and urine becomes concentrated; vasoconstriction at high levels | Stimulated by high plasma osmolarity (osmoreceptors) and low blood volume or pressure (baroreceptors); inhibited by alcohol and ANP |
| Oxytocin | Made in the hypothalamus; released from the posterior pituitary | Peptide | Uterus; mammary glands; brain | Uterine contraction during labor; milk **ejection** (let-down); social bonding | Positive feedback: cervical stretch during labor and suckling increase its release |

<Callout type="trap" title="Production versus ejection">
Prolactin (anterior pituitary) makes milk; oxytocin (posterior pituitary) ejects it. Also, the posterior pituitary releases ADH and oxytocin but doesn't make them. If the hypothalamus is damaged, both are lost, even if the pituitary is intact.
</Callout>

## Thyroid

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thyroxine (T₄) and triiodothyronine (T₃) | Thyroid follicular cells | Amine (iodinated tyrosine) | Nearly all cells | Raise basal metabolic rate and heat production; needed for normal growth and nervous-system development; increase sensitivity to catecholamines | Stimulated by TSH; T₃ and T₄ inhibit TRH and TSH. T₄ is the main secreted form; T₃ is more active, and most is made from T₄ in tissues. |
| Calcitonin | Thyroid parafollicular (C) cells | Peptide | Bone; kidney | Lowers blood Ca²⁺: inhibits osteoclasts, increases renal Ca²⁺ excretion | Stimulated by high blood Ca²⁺ |

<Callout type="mnemonic" title="Calcitonin tones down calcium">
Calci**ton**in **ton**es down blood calcium. Parathyroid hormone does the opposite.
</Callout>

## Parathyroid

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parathyroid hormone (PTH) | Parathyroid chief cells | Peptide | Bone; kidney; (indirectly) intestine | Raises blood Ca²⁺: increases bone resorption by osteoclasts (indirectly, through osteoblasts), increases Ca²⁺ reabsorption and phosphate excretion in the kidney, and activates vitamin D in the kidney | Stimulated by low blood Ca²⁺; inhibited by high Ca²⁺ |

## Adrenal Cortex

The adrenal cortex has three zones, each producing a different class of steroid hormone. From outside to inside: zona glomerulosa, zona fasciculata, zona reticularis.

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aldosterone (mineralocorticoid) | Zona glomerulosa | Steroid | Kidney distal tubule and collecting duct | Increases Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ and H⁺ secretion; water follows Na⁺, raising blood volume and pressure without changing plasma osmolarity much | Stimulated by angiotensin II and high blood K⁺ (ACTH has a minor role); inhibited by ANP |
| Cortisol (glucocorticoid) | Zona fasciculata | Steroid | Liver, muscle, adipose, immune cells and many others | Raises blood glucose (gluconeogenesis), breaks down protein and fat for fuel, suppresses inflammation and immune responses | Stimulated by ACTH; stress; daily rhythm peaks in the early morning; inhibits CRH and ACTH |
| Adrenal androgens (DHEA, androstenedione) | Zona reticularis | Steroid | Many tissues (converted to testosterone or estrogen) | Contribute to pubic and axillary hair and libido, especially in females | Stimulated by ACTH |

<Callout type="mnemonic" title="Salt, sugar, sex">
From outside to inside, the three zones make hormones that regulate **salt** (aldosterone, glomerulosa), **sugar** (cortisol, fasciculata) and **sex** (androgens, reticularis). The zone names go **G**, **F**, **R**, in the same order as "**G**o **F**ind **R**ex."
</Callout>

## Adrenal Medulla

The adrenal medulla is essentially a modified sympathetic ganglion. Its chromaffin cells are stimulated by preganglionic sympathetic neurons.

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Epinephrine (adrenaline) | Adrenal medulla (most of its output) | Amine (catecholamine) | Heart, blood vessels, liver, airways, adipose, many tissues (adrenergic receptors) | Fight-or-flight: raises heart rate and contractility, bronchodilation, glycogenolysis and lipolysis, redirects blood to skeletal muscle | Stimulated by sympathetic nervous system activity (acetylcholine from preganglionic neurons); cortisol enhances its synthesis |
| Norepinephrine (noradrenaline) | Adrenal medulla (smaller share); also sympathetic nerve endings | Amine (catecholamine) | Blood vessels and other adrenergic targets | Vasoconstriction and raised blood pressure; similar to epinephrine overall | Stimulated by sympathetic activity |

## Pancreas

The endocrine pancreas consists of the islets of Langerhans, scattered among the exocrine tissue.

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Insulin | β cells | Peptide | Liver, skeletal muscle, adipose | Lowers blood glucose: increases glucose uptake into muscle and fat (via GLUT4), glycogen synthesis, fat and protein synthesis | Stimulated by high blood glucose, amino acids, incretins (GIP, GLP-1) and parasympathetic activity; inhibited by somatostatin and sympathetic activity |
| Glucagon | α cells | Peptide | Liver (mainly) | Raises blood glucose: glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis; promotes ketone production | Stimulated by low blood glucose, amino acids and sympathetic activity; inhibited by insulin, high glucose and somatostatin |
| Somatostatin | δ cells | Peptide | α and β cells; digestive tract | Inhibits insulin and glucagon secretion; slows digestion and absorption | Stimulated by high blood glucose and amino acids |

<Callout type="trap" title="Glucagon and glycogen">
Glucagon raises blood glucose by breaking down glycogen and making new glucose. Students sometimes mix up the names: **glycogen** is the stored polymer, **glucagon** is the hormone that mobilizes it. Note too that muscle has no glucagon receptors; glucagon acts mainly on the liver.
</Callout>

## Gonads and Placenta

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Testosterone | Testes (Leydig cells) | Steroid | Many tissues; Sertoli cells | Male sexual development and secondary sex characteristics; supports spermatogenesis; increases muscle mass | Stimulated by LH; inhibits GnRH and LH |
| Estrogens (mainly estradiol) | Ovaries (granulosa cells of developing follicles); placenta in pregnancy | Steroid | Uterus, breast, bone, brain and others | Female secondary sex characteristics; thickening of the endometrium (proliferative phase); a sustained high level triggers the LH surge | Stimulated by FSH and LH; negative feedback at most levels, positive feedback at high levels mid-cycle |
| Progesterone | Corpus luteum; placenta in pregnancy | Steroid | Uterus; breast | Maintains and prepares the endometrium for implantation (secretory phase); supports pregnancy | Stimulated by LH (and by hCG in early pregnancy); inhibits GnRH, FSH and LH |
| Inhibin | Sertoli cells; ovarian granulosa cells | Peptide | Anterior pituitary | Inhibits FSH release | Stimulated by FSH |
| Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) | Placenta (trophoblast) | Peptide (glycoprotein) | Corpus luteum | Maintains the corpus luteum, and so its progesterone, in early pregnancy; detected by pregnancy tests | Rises rapidly in the first trimester, then falls as the placenta takes over progesterone production |

## Other Endocrine Tissues

### Pineal gland, kidney and heart

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Melatonin | Pineal gland | Amine (from tryptophan, via serotonin) | Brain and other tissues | Regulates circadian rhythms and promotes sleep | Released in darkness; suppressed by light, via the suprachiasmatic nucleus |
| Erythropoietin (EPO) | Kidney (peritubular interstitial cells) | Peptide (glycoprotein) | Bone marrow | Stimulates red blood cell production | Stimulated by low tissue oxygen (hypoxia), as at high altitude or in anemia |
| Renin | Kidney (juxtaglomerular cells) | Peptide (an enzyme) | Angiotensinogen in the blood | Cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I; ACE (mainly in the lungs) converts it to angiotensin II, which constricts vessels, stimulates aldosterone, ADH and thirst | Stimulated by low renal perfusion pressure, low NaCl delivery to the macula densa and sympathetic activity |
| Calcitriol (active vitamin D) | Kidney (final activation step) | Steroid (secosteroid) | Intestine; bone; kidney | Increases intestinal absorption of Ca²⁺ and phosphate | Activation stimulated by PTH and low phosphate |
| Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) | Heart (atrial muscle cells) | Peptide | Kidney; blood vessels; adrenal cortex | Lowers blood volume and pressure: increases Na⁺ and water excretion, dilates vessels, inhibits renin, aldosterone and ADH | Stimulated by atrial stretch (high blood volume) |

<Callout type="note" title="Two hormones, opposite jobs">
Aldosterone and ANP act on blood volume in opposite directions. Aldosterone is released when blood pressure is low and makes the kidney hold on to sodium and water. ANP is released when the atria are stretched by high volume and makes the kidney excrete sodium and water.
</Callout>

### Gastrointestinal hormones and others

| Hormone | Source | Chemical class | Target | Main effect | Regulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gastrin | G cells of the stomach (antrum) | Peptide | Parietal cells; stomach muscle | Stimulates HCl secretion and gastric motility | Stimulated by peptides in the stomach, stomach distension and vagal input; inhibited by low gastric pH |
| Secretin | S cells of the duodenum | Peptide | Pancreas; stomach; liver | Stimulates pancreatic bicarbonate secretion to neutralize acid; reduces gastric acid secretion and emptying | Stimulated by acid entering the duodenum |
| Cholecystokinin (CCK) | I cells of the duodenum and jejunum | Peptide | Gallbladder; pancreas; brain | Gallbladder contraction (bile release), pancreatic enzyme secretion, satiety; slows gastric emptying | Stimulated by fats and proteins in the small intestine |
| Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP) | K cells of the duodenum and jejunum | Peptide | Pancreatic β cells | Increases insulin secretion (an incretin) | Stimulated by glucose and fat in the intestine |
| Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) | L cells of the ileum and colon | Peptide | Pancreas; stomach; brain | Increases insulin secretion, reduces glucagon, slows gastric emptying, promotes satiety | Stimulated by nutrients in the intestine |
| Ghrelin | Stomach | Peptide | Hypothalamus; anterior pituitary | Increases hunger; stimulates GH release | Rises before meals (fasting); falls after eating |
| Leptin | Adipose tissue | Peptide | Hypothalamus | Reduces appetite; signals long-term energy stores | Released in proportion to fat mass |
| Thymosin | Thymus | Peptide | Developing T cells | Promotes T-cell maturation | Most active in childhood; the thymus shrinks after puberty |

<Callout type="mnemonic" title="Gastrin goes; secretin and CCK slow things down">
Gastrin speeds up acid secretion and motility in the stomach. Secretin and CCK, released when chyme reaches the duodenum, slow the stomach and call in the pancreas and gallbladder to deal with what has arrived.
</Callout>
